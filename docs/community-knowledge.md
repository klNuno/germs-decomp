# What the community already knows

<sub>[germs-decomp](../README.md) / Community knowledge</sub>

What players, wikis and earlier projects knew about Germs: Nerawareta Machi (SLPS-02107, KAJ, 1999-07-22) when this project started, gathered on 2026-11-16. "Code" means read in the game's code or data by an earlier project; "observed" means seen by players; "rumour" means said without evidence anyone can check. Nothing here is trusted until this project's own code reading confirms it; where the code already answers, the row says "Our check" and links to [discoveries.md](discoveries.md). Open questions are in [mysteries.md](mysteries.md).

Source keys used in the tables (full URLs in [Sources](#sources)): **fandom** (the Germs fandom wiki, page named after it), **jawiki** (Japanese Wikipedia), **atwiki** (Game Catalog @Wiki), **HG101** (Hardcore Gaming 101), **GameFAQs** (the FAQ by whowasphone404, v2 of 2025-01-07), **hiddenblur** (the Germs-english translation project's docs), **pastebin** (DuckStation cheat file linked from Romhack Plaza), **nico** (niconico video descriptions, by video id), **manual** (as transcribed on the fandom Manual page).

## Game rules

| Topic | Known | Status | Source |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Clock | Game time runs 6 times faster than real time; the clock (day, hour, minute) is always on screen | observed | jawiki, GameFAQs how-to-play |
| Clock period | A cheat writes 0x0258 (600) to 0x801E6DB0 as "normal" time, 2 as fast, 0xFFFF as stopped. Our check: 600 is the number of vertical blanks between two clock events, one game minute; the day counter stops at day 99. Both seen in the emulator ([the clock](discoveries.md#-the-clock-counts-minutes-and-stops-at-day-99)) | code | pastebin TimeControl |
| Time skips | The 24-hour chiropractor can pass time until the next event; the office sofa allows a nap up to twice a day; the hotel lets you sleep. Our check: the chiropractor sells three courses of 1, 3 and 6 hours that take 60, 180 or 360 points of damage off every body part; with no wound they only pass the time, seen in the emulator ([the chiropractor](discoveries.md#-the-chiropractor-sells-time-1-3-or-6-hours-that-heal-as-much)). Its door tests no hour, so it is open around the clock ([opening hours](discoveries.md#-only-three-shops-close-at-night-the-opening-hours-sit-in-the-towns-door-scripts)) | observed | jawiki, GameFAQs buildings |
| Invasion progress | Each building has its own timeline: before invasion, invaded, freed, and "whole city at peace"; the office computer's city overview shows a state 0 to 4 per building (4 if you have "changed"). Our check: confirmed; the five states are the values 0, any other (usually 100), 10, 1000 and 2000 of one game variable per building, which also picks the version of the building that loads, seen in the emulator ([invasion state](discoveries.md#-each-buildings-invasion-state-is-one-number-and-the-computers-dots-show-it)). The clock never moves them: five buildings fall from a set day and hour when you pass the office's town door or their own ([invasion schedule](discoveries.md#-time-alone-never-spreads-the-invasion-buildings-fall-on-a-schedule-when-you-pass-a-door)) | observed | manual p.2, fandom Indenco |
| No fixed order | No set order of play; an action in one building can set a flag that changes another building's story. Our check: confirmed; freeing one building invades or frees others in a chain written in the scene scripts, alongside five buildings that fall on a schedule ([invasion schedule](discoveries.md#-time-alone-never-spreads-the-invasion-buildings-fall-on-a-schedule-when-you-pass-a-door)) | observed | manual p.2, jawiki |
| Emails | 32 emails; some arrive at fixed times (Naomi day 1 21:00, Sakaguchi day 1 17:00, Indenco days 1 to 3 at 10:00, a mine email day 4 12:00), others after quest steps. Our check: the timed ones are a list in the code; it confirms these four times and adds day 2 at 16:00 and day 3 at 14:00; of the other twenty, three are there at a new game and seventeen are sent by story scenes, each mail by one sender ([the timed mails](discoveries.md#-the-timed-mails-are-written-in-the-code-for-days-1-to-4)) | observed | fandom Emails |
| Stats | HP (healed at the hospital or the chiropractor), fatigue (sleep or nap), stamina (food); stamina at 0 kills you; high fatigue makes stamina fall faster; being hit lowers stamina. Our check: stamina is one energy value that is your life; fatigue is an hours-awake counter that multiplies its drain; only head hits take energy ([energy](discoveries.md#-energy-drains-faster-the-longer-you-stay-awake), [energy at 0](discoveries.md#-energy-at-0-is-death-and-only-a-hit-to-the-head-makes-you-wake-up-changed)). The hospital and the chiropractor heal the body parts, never the energy bar ([the hospital](discoveries.md#-the-hospital-heals-every-wound-at-once-for-free-and-without-losing-time), [the chiropractor](discoveries.md#-the-chiropractor-sells-time-1-3-or-6-hours-that-heal-as-much)). HP is the damage of the five body parts; the status page shows it and the hours awake, never the energy, seen in the emulator ([the status page](discoveries.md#-the-status-page-shows-your-wounds-and-hours-awake-never-your-energy)) | observed | jawiki, GameFAQs how-to-play |
| Energy bar | The fandom wiki describes one "health" bar that drains slowly and is refilled by eating; a heartbeat plays when it is low. GLADIATOR's infinite energy code writes 200 to 0x801E0710. Our check: one energy value at 0x801E0710 drains a little each tick, faster the longer you stay awake, and a low-energy warning starts below about a quarter ([energy](discoveries.md#-energy-drains-faster-the-longer-you-stay-awake)) | observed | fandom Player, psxdatacenter |
| Body parts | Five body parts take damage separately (colour green, red, then blue); the chiropractor heals them; leg damage slows walking and running. The cheat "InfLimbHealth" zeroes five halfwords at 0x801E041C to 0x801E0424 (left arm, torso, legs, right arm, head, per its author). Our check: the order is head, arm, arm, torso, legs (not the cheat author's), with a yellow level between green and red; parts heal 4 points a game hour except at the worst level, seen in the emulator ([body parts](discoveries.md#-body-part-damage-heals-by-the-hour-unless-it-is-at-the-worst-level)). Leg damage slows walking to 90 % at red and 70 % at blue, measured in the emulator; the arm on the left of the figure also lowers the hit chance ([hurt legs and arm](discoveries.md#-hurt-legs-slow-you-down-and-one-hurt-arm-spoils-your-aim)) | observed | fandom Player, manual, pastebin |
| Food | No money: every restaurant, deli and fast-food place is free; eating too much makes you sick and hurts you; take-away food can be eaten anywhere indoors. Our check: confirmed, the shop code reads no money; the sickness comes from eating on a full bar, costs half the dish's value and puts its whole value on the torso, seen in the emulator ([food](discoveries.md#-food-adds-a-fixed-amount-of-energy-and-eating-on-a-full-bar-hurts-your-torso)) | observed | atwiki, GameFAQs how-to-play, fandom Player |
| Combat | No hitbox: a marker appears over a target in range, and pressing attack rolls against the weapon's accuracy; each hit raises that weapon's accuracy. Our check: a roll of 0 to 99 against the hit chance, cut by range; a hit adds twice its damage after defence to the weapon class's experience (none when the defence cancels it), and the class level rises only when that experience crosses a threshold; the level raises the rate only up to what the weapon asks for; the result blinks beside the target mark as HIT or Miss, seen in the emulator ([the hit roll](discoveries.md#-every-shot-is-a-roll-against-the-hit-chance-and-range-cuts-both-chance-and-damage)) | observed | jawiki, atwiki, GameFAQs |
| Weapon experience | Each weapon class has experience shown on a blue wheel; two weapons of one type share it. A cheat sets five 32-bit class values at 0x801E0724 to 0x801E0737 to 0x3B2A005D, which it calls one point below the maximum. Our check: corrected; the blue wheel is the current weapon's hit rate, each class value is a level (1 to 100) and the experience toward the next, `0x3B2A005D` is level 93, and a class holds one to five weapons ([the blue wheel](discoveries.md#-the-blue-wheel-is-the-weapons-hit-rate-and-the-five-classes-are-levels-up-to-100)) | observed | fandom Weapons, pastebin |
| Weapons carried | Two at a time; the rest are left with the assistant at the office. Our check: confirmed; the gun shop refuses a third weapon, and only the office storage takes one off your hands, never your last ([the gun shop](discoveries.md#-the-gun-shop-has-three-fixed-lists-of-six-and-every-weapon-it-never-sells-has-one-giver)) | observed | jawiki, fandom Weapons |
| Weapon list | 18 weapons: 13 ordinary (Baton to Boomerang) and 5 specials (Cloud Buster, Electromagnetic Wave gun, Gravity Field weapon, Crystal, Hieronymus Machine). Our check: the baton is the weapon every new game starts with; the shop sells eleven, and each of the other six has one giver in the story ([the gun shop](discoveries.md#-the-gun-shop-has-three-fixed-lists-of-six-and-every-weapon-it-never-sells-has-one-giver)) | observed | fandom Weapons |
| Gun shop WILD ARMS | Sells six weapons at a time, chosen by your level (0 to 19, 20 to 39, 40 and up); one purchase at a time. Our check: confirmed; the bands are the player level divided by 20, with no fourth, and each band always shows the same six weapons, seen in the emulator. One at a time because you have two hands and no money: with both full the shop refuses, and nothing can be sold back ([the gun shop](discoveries.md#-the-gun-shop-has-three-fixed-lists-of-six-and-every-weapon-it-never-sells-has-one-giver)) | observed | fandom Weapons, atwiki, manual |
| Unobtainable weapons | "A normal player would never see some of the weapons" because of how the shop works; a cheat scrolls weapon slots 0x00 to 0x11 at 0x801E0774 and 0x801E0776. Our check: partly busted. The shop's table never holds the baton, the M47 Dragon or the five special weapons, but the baton is in every player's hand from the start and the other six are story gifts, each given by one script under its own condition: Kouda's tally of 7 for the M47 Dragon, Fujita's mails for two of the specials; what leads to the other three was not traced ([the gun shop](discoveries.md#-the-gun-shop-has-three-fixed-lists-of-six-and-every-weapon-it-never-sells-has-one-giver)) | rumour | Romhack Plaza comment of 2025-04-05, pastebin |
| Minimum weapons | The Self Defense Pistol and the Electromagnetic Wave weapon are the minimum to finish the game. Our check: both can come from Fujita: the pistol as a free gift on the first visit to his house while the power plant is still invaded, only with the second hand empty or holding the baton, and with `PSG` freed first only if `PSG` already gave the back-door map (it is also sold under level 20), the wave gun once his mail has come; whether they are the minimum was not checked ([the gun shop](discoveries.md#-the-gun-shop-has-three-fixed-lists-of-six-and-every-weapon-it-never-sells-has-one-giver)) | observed | fandom Weapons |
| Killing civilians | Anyone can be killed, shop staff included; important NPCs come back; too many kills gets you arrested. Our check: 3 civilian kills give the first arrest, the next time the office loads; mutants never count ([arrests](discoveries.md#-three-civilian-kills-get-you-arrested-and-five-more-the-second-time)) | observed | manual p.2, GameFAQs, jawiki |

## Bugs and oddities

| Topic | Known | Status | Source |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Boss music | Killing a boss before entering its dialogue radius skips the boss music, which can then start after the boss is dead. Our check, for four buildings (power plant, `PSG`, cathedral, warehouses): corrected; the boss track starts with the boss's speech, the boss cannot be fought before it, and in these four leaving the speech spot switches the spot off, so the speech runs once per visit. At the cathedral a death fades the track only if the building's state was 0 to 40 before it: from 80, at the third death; from 100, at none. A later visit that loads the speech's version brings the bosses back alive, and the speech turns them hostile again, at the cathedral and, in part, the power plant; the track never starts again over a dead boss. It was heard after the cathedral's fade only with the fade and the speech forced. The other bosses were not checked ([boss music](discoveries.md#-boss-music-starts-with-the-bosss-speech-once-per-visit-and-comes-back-only-with-a-living-boss)) | observed | fandom Soundtrack |
| Komuro fight | The coal mine music keeps playing under the boss music. Our check: confirmed by ear in the emulator; mine level 1 has no tune, its "music" is its ambience drone, held on a voice of its own, and the boss track plays over it at about the same loudness, heard for 61.7 seconds from the speech; nothing in the code stops the drone before you leave, so it should last the whole fight, which was not played; no stop call is skipped. The boss's card names Komuro ([the mine's drone](discoveries.md#-mine-level-1-has-no-tune-and-its-drone-plays-on-under-the-komuro-boss-track)) | observed | fandom Soundtrack |
| Car stuck | The car catches on kerbs and tunnel edges; only a phone call frees it. Our check: corrected; walls and tunnel sides stop it dead, kerbs only slow it, and backing up frees it, seen in the emulator ([the car](discoveries.md#-the-car-climbs-any-kerb-but-stops-dead-when-a-corner-leaves-the-ground)) | observed | atwiki |
| Analog pad | With the pad in analog mode no button works, because the BIOS pad routine the game uses only fills its button word for digital pads and neGcon. Our check: confirmed in the emulator; with an emulated pad in analog mode no button reaches the game, because the console's routine copies the buttons only for pad types 0x41 and 0x23, and switching back to digital restores control; the game never asks the pad type ([the pad](discoveries.md#-the-game-never-asks-what-kind-of-pad-is-plugged-in)) | code | hiddenblur controls.md |
| Opening fade | The office fades in at an exact narration index (426 bytes for the opening, 386 for the ending); a text that steps over or stops short of it leaves the screen black | code | hiddenblur disc-survey.md |
| Slowdown | Players advise running DuckStation's CPU at 190 %. Our check: the game measures each frame and scales movement by it, 30 frames a second at best while its frame wait completes, so a slow emulator makes it choppy. In DuckStation 0.1-12640 the coal mine with mutants runs at 20 frames a second at 100 % and at 30 from 140 % (15 in PCSX-Redux). 190 % clears it, but it was the first setting tested where Sony's frame wait gave up, in the office, which then ran at about 40 frames a second (faster animations there are a prediction from the code); the mine had no timeout even at 200 %. 140 % to 185 % avoided it in the two scenes tested. The real console was not timed ([frame timing](discoveries.md#-the-game-times-every-frame-so-a-slow-machine-makes-it-choppy-not-slow)) | observed | Romhack Plaza comment |
| Misspelling | The game's katakana for the Hieronymus machine swaps two syllables of the name | observed | jawiki, nico sm1148359 |

## Technical

- The game is one PS-X EXE, `SLPS_021.07`: loaded at 0x80170000, 0x70000 bytes, entry 0x801C8840, `SYSTEM.CNF` with TCB 4, EVENT 16, STACK 0x801FFF00 (hiddenblur disc-survey.md). It links Psy-Q libraries (LoadImage, StoreImage, Krom2RawAdd, PadRead are named there).
- Disc: one Mode 2 data track, 80,788 sectors, 75 files, volume `GERMS001`, no XA audio, no LibCrypt (Redump, hiddenblur). Files: `SCE.S` (7.0 MB, 24 scenes of script and sound), `RES.R` (3.8 MB, 15 blocks, 209 TIMs, a `WEPN` table of 18 records of 0x60 bytes), `C/CITY.*` (27 MB of town), per-place `B`, `F`, `M` files, and `M.STR` and `D.STR` (48 MB each, byte-identical).
- Text: no font of its own; the game draws the BIOS Kanji ROM glyphs through four routines (hiddenblur). Office computer mails are pictures (240x128 4bpp TIMs in `RES.R`), not text.
- Script: `SCE.S` commands are size-prefixed with string arguments; opcode 0x05 carries text; `AREA` chunks hold 12-byte triggers, `ASET` event records, `ENMY` and `ACTR` chunks (hiddenblur, partly decoded).
- Controls: digital pad only; d-pad turns and looks, Triangle and Cross move, L1 and R1 strafe, L2 or R2 attack, Select opens status and map (hiddenblur controls.md). GameFAQs writes "D-pad: Move" and "Triangle: Advance". Our check: L2 or R2 attacks, L2 held with Triangle runs in the town, and SQUARE is never read anywhere ([no cheat code](discoveries.md#-no-cheat-code-and-no-debug-menu-the-only-developer-leftover-is-a-crash-report-no-retail-console-shows)); in the emulator SELECT opened the town map in the street ([the town's size](discoveries.md#-the-town-is-a-grid-of-50-m-blocks-about-48-km-across)) and the weapon panel inside a building ([the weapon record](discoveries.md#-the-weapon-record-three-requirements-accuracy-damage-and-three-ranges)).
- HUD clock: one 96x32 picture whose seven-segment digits are lit by rewriting palette entries (hiddenblur).

## Where sources disagree

- Town size: 2.5 km square on the back insert and in the KAJ brochure, 3 km square in the manual, Japanese Wikipedia and GameFAQs, "about 3 square kilometers" on the fandom wiki. Settled by the town's map file: the area is that of a 2.5 km square, the extent about 4.8 km ([the town's size](discoveries.md#-the-town-is-a-grid-of-50-m-blocks-about-48-km-across)).
- The ending: "no ending" (atwiki, the uploader of nico sm1123083) or "no real ending" (HG101), against a closing narration followed by free play in a half-closed town (fandom, GameFAQs, the 2025 Big Picture Mode essay). The scene id 2 that hiddenblur reads as the ending suggests the narration exists. Settled by the code and the emulator: the narration plays, then free play in a town set to "at peace" ([the ending](discoveries.md#-the-game-has-an-ending-a-narration-in-the-office-then-a-town-at-peace)).
- The movie: two craft through a canyon and into space (atwiki), an alien spacecraft over another planet (HG101), two fighter planes through structures (GameFAQs). All agree it cannot be seen in the game.
- Controls: GameFAQs lists "D-pad: Move" and "Triangle: Advance"; hiddenblur's reading of the movement code says the d-pad turns and looks while Triangle and Cross move forward and back.
- The energy bar: the fandom wiki calls it health refilled by eating; Japanese Wikipedia and GameFAQs separate HP, fatigue and stamina, with stamina falling faster when fatigue is high. The code has one energy value drained by time awake and body damage ([energy](discoveries.md#-energy-drains-faster-the-longer-you-stay-awake)); fatigue is the hours-awake counter; whether the status screen shows HP as a separate value is open.
- `0x801E0710`: GLADIATOR's code treats it as energy; hiddenblur's controls doc names it the structure the movement code receives. Both hold: energy is the first field of that structure.
- Years away: the protagonist comes back after 20 years (Japanese Wikipedia) or 12 years (fandom Naomi Scully page).
- Death: you wake up as a mutant in the mine (fandom, GameFAQs), while stamina at 0 kills outright (Japanese Wikipedia); whether the second also leads to the mine is not said. Settled by the code and the emulator: both hold, and only a recent head hit from an enemy leads to the mine ([energy at 0](discoveries.md#-energy-at-0-is-death-and-only-a-hit-to-the-head-makes-you-wake-up-changed)).
- Developer: KAJ on the disc and in Japanese sources, "KEJ" on psxdatacenter.
